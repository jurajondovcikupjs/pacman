In the original arcade game, Pac-Man ghosts move using deterministic mathematical rules rather than random paths, with each ghost pursuing a specific target tile on the maze grid.

How Navigation Works

• Grid-Based Decisions. The maze is divided into a grid of square tiles, and ghosts look one tile ahead to decide which way to turn at the next intersection.
• Shortest Distance Rule. At an intersection, a ghost calculates the straight-line distance from each available adjacent tile to its target tile and picks the direction that gets it closest.
• No Reversing. Ghosts cannot reverse their direction unless the game forces a global mode change.
• Tie-Breaking Priority. If two directions offer the exact same straight-line distance to the target, the ghost chooses in a strict priority order: Up, Left, Down, Right.

Individual Ghost Personalities (Chase Mode)

During the main Chase mode, each of the four ghosts uses a unique formula to pick its target tile:

Blinky	Red     Targets Pac-Man's exact current tile.	                        The Chaser. Relentlessly follows directly behind you.
Pinky	Pink	Targets 4 tiles ahead of Pac-Man's facing direction

The Three Global Behavior Modes

All four ghosts switch between three behavioral states throughout a level, forcing a 180-degree turn whenever they transition between Chase and Scatter:
• Chase Mode. Ghosts actively hunt Pac-Man using their individual targeting rules above.
• Scatter Mode. Ghosts temporarily stop chasing and retreat toward their respective home corners outside the maze boundaries to give the player brief breathing room.
• Frightened Mode. Triggered when Pac-Man eats an Energizer pellet, turning ghosts dark blue and making them pick turns randomly at intersections using a pseudo-random number generator.
